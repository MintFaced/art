Patrimora

/ˌpatrɪˈmɔːrə/ noun
1.

A fold of the geodetic, taken once and not repeatable. The surface is asked to hold a position it has already left.

Every Patrimora is folded from the same ground and none of them land the same way.

2.

An inheritance whose arrival is deferred but not in doubt. The waiting is part of the thing, not an interruption of it.

He held it as a Patrimora, certain of the estate and unhurried about the date.

From patri‑, of the father, what is handed down, and Latin mora, delay. A heritage postponed but inevitable. The same sentence, in Latin, as the vault.

Patrimora replays the delayed attempts of presence.

Each Patrimora has its own identity.

Each fold transforms the geodetic into Patrimora.
